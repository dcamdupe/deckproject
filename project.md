# Low-Level Timber Deck, Sydney NSW

A planning and design record for a low-level timber deck beside a house. It captures the project brief, the information provided, the decisions made so far, and what is still open. It is intended as the starting context for continuing the work in Claude Code.

## 1. Project summary

- **Location:** Sydney, NSW, Australia.
- **What:** A low-level, single-layer timber deck running alongside the side of a house.
- **First section size:** 1874 mm wide x 3000 mm long.
- **Height:** Less than 1 m above ground (stated by the owner).
- **Structural post:** A 90 x 90 mm post passes through the deck and supports the level above. It is not an edge post for the deck.
- **Future work (the owner has said these come later):**
  - Steps. There will be several, for example a 2 m first deck section, a step down to a 1 m section, then more steps.
  - Deck posts, footings and stirrups.
  - More detail added to the drawing over time.
- **Use case:** A push lawnmower will be moved up and down the steps, so the step edges need protection (ramps were discussed).
- **Reference the owner used:** Bunnings Workshop discussion 45715 (low-level, single-layer deck).

## 3. Decisions made

### 3.1 Framing and layout

- **Joists:** 90 x 45 mm at no more than 450 mm centres.
  - 8 full-length joists plus one short joist (6a) beside the structural post.
  - Joist centre positions: 22.5, 495.5, 923.5, 1351.5, 1779.5, 2207.5, 2555.4 and 2977.5 mm.
  - Joist 6 is set 20 mm clear of the post's top face. Joists 2 to 5 are spread evenly between the joist 1 doubler and joist 6: 383 mm clear gaps, 428 mm centres.
  - Joists 7 and 8 stay on the original 422.14 mm grid. Joist 6 to 7 is 347.9 mm centres.
  - Joist 6a is a short joist 20 mm clear of the post's bottom face (centre 2382.5 mm), running from the central bearer to the right edge, 959.5 mm long.
  - Joist 1 is a double joist: a second 90 x 45 mm member fixed against its inward face (occupying 45 to 90 mm), so 9 joist lengths in total. No other joist moves.
- **Bearers:** 3 bearers, 140 x 45 mm (assumed size), under the joists.
  - Bearer centres: 22.5, 937 and 1851.5 mm, about 914.5 mm apart.
  - This is within the roughly 1000 mm span guide for a 90 x 45 mm joist.
- **Joists sit on top of the bearers** and are continuous. The earlier plan of bearers beside the joists was dropped.
- **Structural post:** The post is not moved to suit the framing. The joists are laid out around it (joist 6 and short joist 6a sit 20 mm either side); no bearer moves.
  - Post occupies x 1526 to 1616 mm from the left and y 2250 to 2340 mm from the top.
  - Clearances: 20 mm to joist 6 (above), 20 mm to joist 6a (below), 213 mm to the right bearer, 566.5 mm to the centre bearer.
  - The post sits between joists 6 and 6a.
- **Deck posts:** 9 posts, 90 x 90 mm (assumed size), three under each bearer.
  - Along the length: centres at 45, 1500 and 2955 mm from the top, so the bearers span 1455 mm between post centres. This span is not yet checked against AS 1684.
  - Across the width: centres at 45, 937 and 1829 mm from the left. Edge posts are kept flush inside the deck outline rather than centred on the edge bearers.
  - Height, footings and stirrups are still open.
- **Floorboards:** 135 x 20 mm with 2 mm gaps, running the 3000 mm length, across the joists.
  - 14 boards across the 1874 mm width: 13 full boards and a last board on the right-hand side cut down to 93 mm.
  - The structural post (1526 to 1616 mm from the left) falls entirely within board 12 (1507 to 1642 mm), so only that board needs cutting around it.
- **Step edge boards:** each section ends in one 135 mm board laid across the 1874 mm width at the step, at 90 degrees to the other boards. The lengthwise boards stop 2 mm short of it, so they are 2883 mm long in section 1 and 1673 mm in section 2.
  - The edge board runs parallel to the joists. It and the ends of the lengthwise boards need a joist or blocking under them (around 135 mm back from the step). This support is not yet in the layout.
- **Step face boards:** the face of each step is covered with boards running across the width, sitting below the horizontal board above. That board overhangs the frame by 20 mm (one board thickness), so its edge is flush with the front of the face boards.
  - The face below the overhang is 180 mm high: one full 135 mm board and one cut to 43 mm, with a 2 mm gap.
  - The boards of the level below start 20 mm in, against the front of the face boards.

### 3.1a Section 2 (step down)

- **Position:** directly below section 1 in the top view, continuing along the length. Same 1874 mm width (assumed), 1810 mm long.
- **Drop:** 200 mm below section 1, measured between board tops.
- **Joists:** 5 joists, 90 x 45 mm, at 441.25 mm centres (22.5, 463.75, 905, 1346.25 and 1787.5 mm from the top of the section). Joist 5, at the bottom edge of the section where it steps down, is a double joist, with the second member against its inward face (1720 to 1765 mm), so 6 joist lengths in total. No other joist moves.
- **Bearers:** 3 bearers, 140 x 45 mm (assumed), on the same lines as section 1, 1810 mm long.
- **Deck posts:** 9 posts, three per bearer, at 45, 905 and 1765 mm along the section, so the bearers span 860 mm. Section 2 has its own posts at the step; they are not shared with section 1's end posts.
- **Floorboards:** same 14-board layout as section 1, plus its own step edge board and face boards.
- **Not yet decided:** whether the two sections share posts at the junction.

### 3.1b Steps down to the ground

- **Total drop:** 1000 mm from section 1 to the ground, in 5 falls of 200 mm: section 1 to section 2, section 2 to step 1, then steps 1, 2 and 3, with the last fall landing on the ground.
  - The owner first gave 1070 mm as the total. Because the remaining 870 mm is not a multiple of 200, the owner chose to keep 200 mm falls and stop at 1000 mm, so the ground at the foot of the steps needs building up by about 70 mm (for example a paver or slab).
- **Steps:** 3 steps below section 2, each 1874 mm wide with a 272 mm going (two 135 mm boards and a 2 mm gap, so no board is ripped).
- **Step boards:** two boards laid across the width on each step, in the same direction as the section edge boards, and face boards (135 mm and 43 mm) on each face, tucked under the overhanging board above. Each step's boards are shifted 20 mm forward to make that overhang, so the going is still 272 mm.
- **Stringers:** the steps are carried by 5 cut stringers at 457.25 mm centres across the width (412 mm clear between them), in place of the joists, bearers and posts first drawn under each step.
  - 457.25 mm is 7 mm over the 450 mm limit used for the joists; the owner accepted this (11 October 2026) to use 5 stringers, not 6.
  - Stock is assumed to be 290 x 45 mm. With a 200 mm fall and 272 mm going each notch is about 161 mm deep, leaving about 129 mm under the notches. A 240 mm board would leave only about 79 mm. This is geometry only, not a span-table check.
  - Top: the stringers butt against a 190 x 45 mm ledger (assumed) across the width, fixed to the faces of section 2's three end posts, because the stringers start below section 2's joists.
  - Bottom: the stringer feet land on a concrete pad or strip footing at the ground, held off the soil on brackets. The footing size is not set.
  - There are no double joists on the steps; the face boards fix to the stringer risers.
- **Reference:** `stringer-example.svg` is a labelled side view of the stringer idea.
- **Option: pre-made steel stringers.** [Peak Products Black 3 Tread Steel Stair Stringer (Bunnings)](https://www.bunnings.com.au/peak-products-black-3-tread-steel-stair-stringer_p0083611) could replace the cut timber stringers, using 3 of them across the 1874 mm width (one at each side and one in the middle, about 915 mm apart). Not adopted yet; the drawing still shows 5 timber stringers.
  - Listed geometry is a 165 mm rise and 240 mm going, against the 200 mm and 272 mm drawn, so the steps would need re-setting out. 165 mm is inside the NCC riser range of 115 to 190 mm.
  - Height: 800 mm from section 2 needs about 5 rises at 165 mm (825 mm). The listing does not state the total height of the 3-tread size, so check whether it is tall enough or whether a larger size is needed.
  - Bunnings lists two stringers for stairs up to 1200 mm wide, so three covers the width. That spacing needs solid stair treads (around 45 mm thick); 20 mm decking boards would need support about every 450 mm.
  - A 240 mm going will not take two 135 mm boards, and the listing does not cover fixing face boards.
  - Finish is powder-coated steel, with no mention of galvanising. Load rating, steel thickness and fixing details were not on the listing; check the manufacturer's installation guide.
- **To check:** a 200 mm fall is above the NCC stair riser range of 115 to 190 mm, and a 1000 mm drop is at the limits noted in section 3.3 (exempt development needs a deck floor no more than 1 m high, and a barrier is required where the fall is 1 m or more).

### 3.2 Posts, footings and treatment

- **Stirrups:** Bolt-down or cast-in stirrups are the approach being considered for deck posts, with the stirrup at least 75 mm above finished ground.
  - An earlier statement of 30 to 50 mm above concrete was wrong and has been corrected.
- **Treatment class:** H4 versus H5 was discussed. H5 is the heavier treatment for in-ground or severe-decay contact. Check AS 1604 and the supplier's tags before buying.
- **Posts against a house:** The question of attaching to the house versus concreting posts in was discussed for the deck's supporting posts, not the structural post.

### 3.3 Approvals and clearances

- The owner says the deck is under 1 m. In NSW the deck floor height must be at most 1 m, among other criteria, to qualify as exempt or complying development. Check the NSW Planning Portal for the full criteria.
- Under-floor clearance and subfloor ventilation are driven by the NCC (150 mm clearance, more for termite inspection). This still needs to be confirmed for this site.

### 3.4 Drawing conventions

- Top view on the left. Side view on the right, looking from the right-hand edge with up to the left, so joist ends are nearest the top view and the bearer is beyond.
- Both views share the vertical scale so the joist rows align.
- Section 2 is drawn below section 1 in the top view, and shifted right by its drop in the side view.
- Nothing in the drawing is configurable unless the owner asks. The only toggles are Joists, Bearers, Deck posts, Floorboards and Structural post.
- No inputs and no layers for joist numbers, dimensions or bearer lines.

## 4. The interactive drawing

An interactive SVG page was built and published as a private artifact (Version 6). Its source is a single HTML file with a JavaScript IIFE that generates the SVG.

- **Features:** top and side views, show/hide toggles (Joists, Bearers, Deck posts, Floorboards, Structural post), hover/tap/focus readouts for joists, bearers, deck posts and the structural post, summary chips, light and dark themes, IBM Plex fonts, aria labels.
- **Key constants:**

```js
var P = { W: 1874, L: 3000, t: 45, d: 90, maxC: 450, nb: 3, bt: 45, bd: 140, ps: 90, pt: 2250, pr: 258, pg: 20, dbl: [0], dps: 90, npp: 3, bw: 135, bh: 20, bg: 2 };
/* P2 copies P for section 2 with L: 1810, pt: null (no structural post) and dbl: [4] */
/* PS copies P for each step with L: GOING, pt: null and dbl: [] */
var ST = { ns: 5, sd: 290, ld: 190, pad: 80, maxC: 460 }; /* stringers: count, stock depth, ledger depth, footing depth, accepted max centres */
var FALL = 200, GOING = 272, NSTEPS = 3;
var K = 0.27, OX = 176, OY = 74, SX = 748, VBW = 1040, GUIDE_SPAN = 1000;
```

- **Layout maths:**
  - Joist grid: `n = ceil((L - t) / maxC)`, centres `c = (L - t) / n`. The last grid joist above the post is then moved to `pg` clear of it, and the joists before it are spaced with equal clear gaps.
  - Bearer spacing: `s = (W - bt) / (nb - 1)`.
  - Post left edge: `postX = W - pr - ps = 1526`.
- **Not yet checked:** the rendered page has never been inspected visually. Its script passed a syntax check only.

## 5. Assumptions and caveats

- Joist and bearer sizes and spans come from community guidance (joists at 450 mm centres or closer, a 90 x 45 mm joist spanning about 1000 mm). They are not taken from AS 1684 span tables.
- Bearer size (140 x 45 mm) is an assumption.
- Before building, confirm member sizes, spans, fixings and footings against AS 1684 and the supplier's span tables, or with a builder or engineer.
- Confirm the load path for the 90 x 90 mm structural post (what it carries and what it bears on). This is not the deck framing's job, and the post should not rely on the deck for support.
- Council and planning rules should be confirmed on the NSW Planning Portal for the actual site.
